# WP10 — The translator writing brief: one person talking

Owner: Builder · Status: Briefed · Depends on: wp8-2 and wp9 merged to main. Own worktree; recordings shared.

Read `CLAUDE.md` first, then `lib/translator.ts` in full, then this brief. The writing guidance in §2 is Robin's house style, adapted for a presenter who may not add facts.

## Why

The translator has never had a writing brief. Its prompt is a schema and a set of limits, and the lines come out as captions: bitty, self-contained, six separate sentences that never sound like a person. Robin: "it just needs humanising." The fix has two parts — a voice, and a structure that writes the scene before it cuts the beats.

## 1. Structure: write the scene, then cut it

Change the translator's order of work. For each scene:

1. Write the scene's narration as **one flowing passage** in the voice below — 30–45 seconds of a person talking, 2–3 beats' worth, from the cited sentences.
2. Only then mark the beat boundaries inside the passage (each beat still gets `line`, `headline`, `subjects`, `action`, `handoff`, `source`, `delivery`, and the scene keeps `connector` and `tag`).
3. `line` for each beat is the passage's text between its boundaries, verbatim. The passage is stored on the scene as `narration` so it can be checked and voiced as one piece (WP8's per-scene Saskia already does this).

Word budget per beat stays 22; a scene's passage is therefore ≤ 66 words. WP8.2's scene audio budget still splits a scene whose audio exceeds ~14 s.

## 2. The voice (goes into the translator prompt as written)

You are writing for Saskia, a Curation presenter, talking to one person sitting across from her. Not broadcasting.

**Sound like a person.** Use contractions. Vary the rhythm: a short punchy sentence, then a longer one that breathes. Start a sentence with "And" or "But" when it's right. Use a fragment for emphasis. Let a thought trail with an ellipsis when that's how it moves. Say "you" and "we."

**Keep it simple and concrete.** Explain it like you would to a friend over coffee. Relatable images over jargon. Not "achieve operating leverage" but "the costs stop growing as fast as the money coming in." Every figure exact and complete.

**Open with recognition, deliver as discovery.** Start where the listener already is — the thing they'd notice, the number that matters — then reveal what it means. Insight sounds discovered, not declared. Prefer "here's the number that matters" to "it is important to note."

**Clarity over cleverness.** Every word moves the listener forward. No paired-phrase constructions for their own sake ("not this, but that"), no rhetorical yes-and-no, no over-confident illustration. If a line sounds pleased with itself, cut it.

**Avoid:** corporate buzzwords; "one might consider" (say "you might"); "it is important to note" (just note it); "in order to" (to); "due to the fact that" (because); "leverage," "unlock," "landscape," "robust," "ecosystem," "journey," "navigate."

**Two rules that override the style, always:**

- **Style is free; substance is inherited.** Every fact, figure, claim and characterisation comes from the cited sentences. Saskia can say "that's not long" when the answer says "dangerously thin." She cannot say "honestly, I'd be nervous," ever. She has no opinions of her own; she has CurationAI's, said warmly. No "I think," "I'm not sure," or "maybe it's just me" unless the answer itself hedges.
- **One aside per scene, at most, and it has to do work.** A beat of recognition, a "which is…" — then back to the answer. No tangents; every line has a source pointer and a clock.

## 3. The example (goes into the prompt as the exemplar)

Scene 1 (violet), beats 1–2:
> So here's the number that matters. At the end of September, Diginex had one point eight five million in the bank. Six months before that? Three point one one. That's a company spending its way through a transition.

Scene 2 (magenta), beats 3–4:
> And the burn is about one point three million a month. Which means the cash on hand covers roughly one point four months. That's… not long.

Scene 3 (lime), beats 5–6:
> But here's what kept them going: an eleven point four million warrant exercise, and then thirteen point eight million more in October. Diginex lives on the capital markets right now. The next two quarters tell you whether the revenue can start closing that gap on its own.

Every figure is in the cited sentences; "not long" maps to "dangerously thin"; "lives on the capital markets" maps to "survival hinges on continued access to capital markets."

## 4. Delivery tags

The `delivery` tags follow the passage, not the beat: `[presenting to camera]` once at the opener, one `[excited]` or `[fast-paced]` on the scene's turn, nothing on the close. Still ≤ 1 per line, ~3 per programme.

## 5. Checks (`npm run check`)

- `narration` split at beat boundaries equals the beats' `line`s concatenated.
- Every sentence in `narration` maps to a cited sentence (existing source check, applied to the passage).
- Word budget per beat and per scene.
- A short banned-word list from §2 (the *avoid* list) fails the beat; a warning for any first-person opinion marker ("I think," "I'd," "honestly I") — fail if the cited sentence has no hedge.
- Rewrite the pinned exemplars (5 s and 15 s) in the new voice.

## 6. Render and compare

- Spine and the two live translations in scene mode, new voice, palette per current default, and once with `PALETTE=c` for the mono comparison Robin asked for.
- `reels/spine-voice-before-vs-after.mp4` — WP8.2's voice-led spine (old lines) beside the new one; the picture may differ, the point is the ear.
- `briefs/WP10-report.md`: words per beat and per scene; check results; count of asides per scene; Whisper split integrity as before; three example passages from live translations quoted in full with their source sentences beside them.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The translator writes `narration` per scene first and derives `line`s from it; verified in the recording JSON.
2. The voice section and exemplar are in the translator prompt as written in §2–3.
3. `npm run check` enforces §5; passes on the rewritten exemplars.
4. Three live-translation passages in the report read as one person talking and every sentence maps to a cited sentence (reviewer reads them against the answers).
5. Reels exist, including the mono comparison.
6. No beat exceeds 22 words; no scene exceeds 66.
